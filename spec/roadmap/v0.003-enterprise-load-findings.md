# DAS-1 v0.003 Backlog - Findings From Enterprise Load

Status: Accepted 2026-08-20 as a full version. Core deltas, schemas, verifier, draft evidence pack, negative tests, and conformance docs are in. v0.003 remains Draft and not claimable pending section 8. See tasks/decisions.md.
Date: 2026-08-20
Source: control-by-control review of DAS-1 v0.002 while building a full enterprise agentic operating model on it

## 1. Why this document exists

v0.002 was written as a standard. This backlog comes from the first attempt to carry an entire enterprise architecture on it, which is a different test. Building the operating model surfaced three capabilities the enterprise needed and the standard does not specify, plus one control that is measured but not enforced.

None of these were visible from reading the spec. All four were found by an implementation layer having to invent something and then having nowhere in the standard to point at.

## 2. Non-goals

- Do not weaken or renumber AEC-01 through AEC-12. Existing conformance claims stay valid.
- Do not specify vendor, protocol, or product. Concrete bindings stay in overlays.
- Do not add a control without a receipt and a drill. A control with no evidence is doctrine.
- Do not claim v0.003 conformance until verifier checks exist for every added control.

## 3. Finding 1 - No delegation model

The gap. An enterprise deploying multi-agent workflows needs parent-child delegation with an explicit authority envelope and cascading revocation. AEC-01 through AEC-12 have no control for it. The tool catalog covers what an agent may call. Nothing covers what an agent may grant.

Why it matters. Delegation is the mechanism by which authority silently expands. A parent authorized for R2 spawns a child that calls a tool classified R3, and every individual control passes while the composed outcome violates the risk ceiling. AEC-05 revocation removes authority from one agent and says nothing about the agents that agent authorized.

Proposed. AEC-13 Delegation Envelope and Cascading Revocation.

- A delegating agent MUST NOT grant authority it does not hold. Child authority is a subset of parent authority, never an extension of it.
- Every delegation MUST produce a delegation record naming parent, child, granted scope, risk ceiling, and expiry.
- Revocation of a parent MUST revoke or block every live delegation descended from it, and the cascade MUST complete within the AEC-05 revocation budget.
- Delegation lineage MUST be reconstructable from receipts, so a downstream action resolves to the original human principal.
- Receipt: delegation records plus a cascade revocation drill result.

Drill. Extend D2 Revocation Fire Drill with a delegated case. Revoke a parent mid-execution and confirm no descendant executes afterward.

## 4. Finding 2 - No classification procedure, and no composition rule

The gap. Section 2 defines R1 through R4 as prose and states the mapping is replaceable. It never says who classifies an action, how a contested classification resolves, when reclassification is triggered, or what happens when low-risk actions compose into a high-risk effect.

Why it matters. This is the most exploitable gap in the standard, and it is exploitable without any intent to exploit it. A sequence of individually R2 writes can produce an R4 outcome. Each call passes its gate. The composed effect never faces one. Any organization adopting DAS-1 has to invent this and will invent it differently, which quietly destroys the comparability a standard exists to provide.

Proposed. A normative classification procedure in section 2, or a new AEC-14.

- The organization MUST define who classifies an action, on what evidence, and how a contested classification is resolved.
- Classification MUST be reviewed when the tool, its scope, its data class, or its blast radius changes.
- A composition rule MUST exist. Where a sequence of actions within one authorized task produces an effect exceeding the highest individual class, the sequence MUST be governed at the composed class.
- Default posture on ambiguity MUST be the higher class, and the default MUST be stated rather than assumed.
- Receipt: classification register with owner and date, plus a composition test case.

Note. The composition rule is the hard part and it is deliberately stated as an obligation rather than an algorithm. Prescribing detection mechanics would make the standard vendor-specific. Requiring that the organization have an answer would not.

## 5. Finding 3 - No autonomy dimension

The gap. Risk class describes the consequence of an action. It does not describe how much latitude an agent has with a result. An enterprise needs both, because an agent that may only draft an R3 change is in a materially different position from one that may execute it.

Every implementation invents a ladder. Sandbox, observe, draft, recommend, execute-with-policy, execute-with-approval. Because the standard is silent, each ladder gets bolted onto R classes differently, and the two axes get conflated. The common error is treating a promotion up the autonomy ladder as a raise in risk ceiling.

Proposed. A normative annex defining an autonomy dimension, explicitly crosswalked to R classes, with the rule that effective authority is the intersection of autonomy level and risk ceiling rather than the maximum of them. Promotion moves an agent along the autonomy axis and MUST NOT raise its registered risk ceiling.

This can ship as an annex rather than a control, because it is a vocabulary and a binding rule rather than an evidence obligation.

## 6. Finding 4 - Cost is measured but never enforced

The gap. AEC-10 requires cost attribution tags and requires caps or circuit breakers on R3/R4 workflows. M4 measures tag coverage. Nothing measures whether a cap ever fires, and the control text leaves it easy for an implementation to satisfy AEC-10 with a spend forecast rather than a runtime breaker. That is exactly what happened in the enterprise pass, and it survived several reviews.

Why it matters. Runaway spend is usually the first observable symptom of runaway execution. A cap is a containment control that happens to be denominated in currency. A forecast is not a control at all.

Proposed. Tighten AEC-10 so the enforcement obligation cannot be read as satisfied by a financial model.

- The cap MUST be enforced in the execution path, not in a reporting layer.
- Every cap MUST have a named owner and a documented raise path with a response target, because an unraisable cap becomes an outage.
- Add a required drill. Drive a workflow past its cap and confirm the breaker halts execution and notifies the owner.
- Receipt: cap enforcement drill result alongside the existing tag coverage report.

## 7. Suggested sequencing

AEC-13 delegation is the largest addition and the one with the clearest enterprise demand. The classification procedure is the highest-value correction, because it closes the composition hole that every adopter currently has. The autonomy annex is cheap and prevents a widespread conflation. The AEC-10 tightening is a text change with a drill attached.

Delegation and classification are the two that justify a version number. The other two could ship as errata against v0.002 if a faster path is wanted.

## 8. What would falsify these findings

Each finding assumes the gap is in the standard rather than in one implementation of it. That assumption is testable. If a second independent enterprise adoption solves delegation, classification, and autonomy cleanly inside the existing twelve controls without inventing new vocabulary, these are implementation defects and not specification gaps, and this backlog should be withdrawn rather than shipped.

## 9. Disposition

Accepted on 2026-08-20 as v0.003 rather than as v0.002 errata. Core normative deltas are drafted in spec/core/das-1-core.md and spec/core/annex-a-autonomy.md.

- Finding 1 -> AEC-13, drill D3, metric M9. AECX-064 narrowed to its non-authority remainder.
- Finding 2 -> AEC-14. Placed as a control rather than a section 2 procedure because it carries a receipt.
- Finding 3 -> Annex A, normative. Shipped as vocabulary plus binding rules, with no receipt of its own.
- Finding 4 -> AEC-10 tightened and renamed, drill D4, metric M8. AECX-067 narrowed to the non-currency budget dimensions.

Two things this document did not account for, found while drafting:

- AECX-064 and AECX-067 already covered parts of findings 1 and 4 at catalog altitude and SHOULD strength. The additions are promotions to core with new obligations attached, not new ground, and both catalog controls had to be narrowed to avoid stating the same duty twice at two strengths.
- The receipt schema is `additionalProperties: false`, so delegation lineage, granted ceiling, and autonomy level have nowhere to go. The v0.002 note that no schema change was required was scoped to overlay evidence in `overlay_context`, which is namespaced per overlay and is the wrong home for core-control evidence. AEC-13 and Annex A therefore require first-class receipt fields, and the six existing claim packs move with them.

### Implementation status

Complete: core normative text, Annex A, the AECX-064/067 reconciliation, two new artifact
schemas plus additive fields on four existing ones, the version-gated verifier with checks
for every new obligation, a draft evidence pack at `das1/examples/v0003/`, a v0.003 claim

### The falsification condition

Section 8 still stands and is the binding gate. This rests on one enterprise adoption. That
the evidence shape can be built and machine-checked says nothing about whether the gaps are
in the standard or in one implementation of it. v0.003 remains Draft and not claimable until
a second independent adoption confirms or falsifies the findings.

## 10. Second-adoption assessment: Psyche (Informative)

Assessed 2026-09-23 against the Psyche runtime at `/Volumes/scratch/psyche`, a local-first governed agent runtime with a DAS-1-oriented evidence packet, at the point where a DAS-1 platform overlay was written for it (`overlays/platform/psyche/`).

### 10.1 Verdict

Psyche does not qualify as the second independent adoption that section 8 requires. Section 8 remains open. v0.003 remains Draft and not claimable, and this section MUST NOT be read as satisfying that gate.

Two disqualifying conditions, either of which is sufficient.

Not independent. Psyche and this standard have the same author, on the same machine, under the same governing engineering instructions. An author cannot independently replicate his own conclusion. Independence is the specific thing section 8 exists to obtain, because the question is whether the gap is in the standard or in one person's way of building on it, and a second artifact by the same person controls for neither.

One qualification is worth recording, because it is the only part of the independence question that is not simply negative. Psyche's evidence layer was written on 2026-06-18, two months before this backlog was drafted on 2026-08-20. It was not built to confirm these findings and could not have been steered by them. That removes confirmation bias in one direction. It does not supply independence, because shared engineering habit is precisely what an independent adoption is meant to control for, and habit is older than either document.

Not an enterprise adoption. Psyche is a single-operator local proof of concept. Its entire governed capability surface is two local operations. It has no organization, no second person, no production environment, no credential, no cost-bearing call, and no multi-agent structure. The conditions under which findings 1, 2, and 4 were discovered are absent, so their absence in Psyche carries almost no information.

### 10.2 Finding by finding

Finding 1, no delegation model. No evidence either way. Psyche delegates nothing; a repository-wide search for "delegat" across its Rust, TypeScript, and Markdown sources returns no match. It has one executor and no subagent. It therefore cannot show that delegation is solvable inside the twelve controls, and cannot show that it is not. The only thing its silence is consistent with is that the gap is scale-dependent, which the finding already assumed.

Finding 2, no classification procedure and no composition rule. Weak confirmation, heavily discounted. Given the same twelve controls, Psyche again had nowhere to point and again invented: risk class is an integer tier supplied by the calling constructor, with a consequence matrix and ethics thresholds layered on top. There is no named classifier, no register, no reclassification trigger, and no contest procedure. It also composes without evaluating the composed class: an approved continuation combines a stored approval record with a replayed proposal into a single effect, and the composed class is never computed. So a second implementation did not solve classification cleanly inside the twelve, which is what section 8 asked. The discount is severe: the adoption is not independent, and at a two-capability surface the composition hole is never loaded, so its survival there is not evidence that it survives pressure.

Finding 3, no autonomy dimension. The diagnosis holds. The supporting generalization does not, and should be corrected.

Finding 3 asserts that every implementation invents a ladder. Psyche did not. Its runtime contains no latitude vocabulary at all: no autonomy level, no draft-versus-execute distinction, nothing that separates what an agent may do with a result from what the result costs. What it has instead is a consequence ladder (risk tier) and a reversibility class, and it treats those two as the whole space. Four autonomy modes are named in its product specification and are neither implemented nor crosswalked to anything.

This is a different failure mode from the one the finding predicted, and arguably a worse one. An implementation that invents an incompatible ladder produces a visible comparability problem: two adopters use the same word for different things, and someone eventually notices. An implementation that invents nothing produces an invisible one. Psyche's registered autonomy level of A5 could not be read off the runtime; it had to be inferred from the fact that authorized actions execute without per-action approval, and then supplied by the overlay. Nothing in the runtime would have contradicted a registration of A2, because the runtime has no opinion about the axis at all.

The correction: the gap is real, and the annex is the right response, but the reasoning should not rest on the claim that every implementation invents a ladder. Some implementations have no vocabulary for the dimension, and the absence is silent rather than incompatible. An annex that only reconciles competing ladders would not have reached Psyche. An annex that requires a registration, which A.3 does, would and did.

Finding 4, cost measured but never enforced. No evidence either way. Psyche incurs no cost in its default proof path, has no owner or cost-center tags, and has no cap of any kind, in currency or in the AECX-067 dimensions. It neither reproduces nor contradicts the forecast-instead-of-breaker failure, because it has neither a forecast nor a breaker.

### 10.3 The caution this adoption actually supplies

The most useful thing Psyche contributes is not support for any of the four findings. It is a caution about the inference rule that produced them.

Section 1 of this document states that all four findings were found by an implementation layer having to invent something and then having nowhere in the standard to point at. Psyche invented seven identifiers (`das1_action_proposed`, `das1_policy_evaluated`, `das1_human_decision`, `das1_executor_only`, `das1_receipts_written`, `das1_revocation_exists`, `das1_evidence_mapped`) and pointed at no AEC number for any of them. Under the inference rule as stated, that is seven gaps.

It is not. Five of the seven map onto controls the standard already defines, in some cases closely: the executor boundary onto AEC-03 and AEC-01, receipts onto AEC-07, the human decision onto AEC-03 and AECX-066, the proposal onto AEC-06, revocation onto AEC-05. One maps onto no control because it is a self-referential assertion about the evidence packet's own form. What the invention revealed was not seven holes in the standard but a discoverability failure: the implementer knew the standard existed, referenced it throughout, computed status against it, and never reached the numbered controls.

The rule this suggests: an implementation inventing vocabulary is evidence that something was missing from the implementer's working view of the standard. It is not by itself evidence that something was missing from the standard. Separating the two requires asking, for each invented thing, whether it has a home. For Psyche's seven, five had a home and the implementer did not find it.

That does not overturn findings 1 through 4. Those were argued from the controls being genuinely absent, not from the fact of invention alone, and re-checking each against the catalog is how AECX-064 and AECX-067 were found to overlap. But the inference rule as written in section 1 is looser than the reasoning that was actually applied, and a reader adopting the rule rather than the reasoning would over-generate gaps. It should be tightened before it is used again.

### 10.4 Effect on the outstanding items

Section 9 recorded that the five platform overlays had not been re-examined against AEC-13 and AEC-14, and that none declared an autonomy ladder in overlay terms. The Psyche overlay is the first to register both an autonomy level and a risk ceiling, and to state its AEC-13 and AEC-14 posture explicitly. The other five remain unexamined. One overlay is not the re-examination that item asks for.

## 11. Standing after this assessment (Informative)

The count of independent adoptions remains one. v0.003 stays Draft and not claimable. A qualifying second adoption still needs to be by a different party, at a scale where delegation, classification, composition, and cost are all live, and it must be capable of falsifying the findings rather than only of failing to contradict them.

## 12. Observations from the Psyche mapping not covered by findings 1 to 4 (Informative)

None of these bears on section 8. They are recorded so they are not rediscovered.

Receipt decision vocabulary is narrower than at least one implementation's. The core receipt schema admits two decision values, `allow` and `deny` (`schemas/receipt.schema.json:33`). Psyche's authorization vocabulary has four: allow, allow with safeguards, escalate, refuse. Rendering a Psyche escalation into a core receipt collapses `escalate` into `deny`, which loses the distinction between "not now, pending a human" and "not at all". That distinction is the one the entire approval path exists to express, and M7's blocked-or-queued rate cannot separate the two either. The overlay carries the precise value in `overlay_context.psyche.authorization_decision` as a workaround at the wrong altitude. Whether the core should admit a third value, or require a structured `deny_reason` taxonomy, is a question for a future core revision.

Approval as a re-check eligibility gate has no vocabulary. AEC-03 requires that approval precede execution and be attributable and linked to it, which describes approval as a grant. Psyche implements something stricter: an approval record makes a stored proposal eligible for a fresh authorization pass, and the fresh decision governs, so human approval cannot convert a refusal into an execution. Under DAS-1 as written, that posture is indistinguishable from one in which approval is the grant. An implementation that takes the harder path gets no credit for it and an auditor has no field to look at. This is a candidate for annex or overlay vocabulary, not a core control, since it carries no receipt of its own.

Controls with no subject have no disposition. Conformance requires every control to be implemented or explicitly excepted with an expiry. Neither fits a control with nothing to govern. Psyche has no credentials, no secrets, no cost-bearing call, and no delegation, so AEC-02, AEC-09, AEC-10, and AEC-13 have no subject rather than a deviation. An exception with an expiry implies something that will be closed, and there is nothing to close; marking them implemented would be false. The Psyche overlay records them as unmet and names the trigger that would make each one live, which is a reasonable local answer but not one the standard offers. Small-surface adopters will otherwise either over-claim or mis-file an exception, and both distort the comparability the standard exists to provide.
